# PEAK HOUR v3 - balance simulation report

Generated by `tests/sim_balance.py` from `data/balance.json`.
Seeds: **240**, hours: **9**, dt: **0.25 s**, send quality q=**0.85**, attack quality q=**0.85**, protocol: **TCP**; starvation scope: **first**.

**Production variant**: `first` guard, no file-count cap/carry, H1 `income_target` = **100 $**.

## Model assumptions

- Hour length comes from `hours[].duration` (120..360 s). `constants.hour_seconds` is only a legacy fallback when a row has no duration; `dt` is 0.25 s.
- Receive spawn: `hours[].files` is a **rate/expected count**, not a cap. Window = 0.75*duration; interval = window/files with +/-20% jitter; packets spawn for the whole window. There is no per-hour count limit and no carry. H1 spawns its first packet at t=0. A full buffer or no free cells for any available type pauses spawns.
- Types: `hours[].type_weights` only; zero weight never spawns.
- Files are a catalog, not a consumable: a successful send only releases the request link (`req_id=None`). Files stay in the folder, keep their cells occupied and are immediately reusable by later requests. New files keep arriving at the `files` rate; the folder is never capped by a per-hour file budget.
- Requests: no `request_cap`; only `active_requests` and the cutoff `elapsed >= duration - 10`. Interval `hours[].send_interval`. `first`: the guard may spawn only the first request of the hour (`requests_spawned == 0`); after that `send_interval` is respected. A request randomly claims 1-4 free files (`request_mix`) for its whole lifetime. H1 also seeds one extra text file + one request at t=0 (production bootstrap).
- Player: competent, attaches after 1.5 s + 1 s/file, plays a no-timer send minigame for 7 + 1.5*(tier+1) seconds (3 tiers). TCP adds 4.2 s connection time and 4% of attempts miss it (-4 SLA). The minigame pauses packet spawn/loading but not other request timers (production order).
- Timers: request `30 + 7*timer_level` (cap 65), packet 20 s. Request timers pause in the player's own minigame, in the out-slot queue and during the send; payout uses `time_left` snapshotted at send start.
- Overflow: a waiting packet with `occupied + cells > capacity` is not loaded, its timer does not tick and it is not fined; spawn pauses when free cells are 0 (or no available type fits). No SLA penalty is applied for being unable to accept.
- Transfers: `base_time_sec * 0.90^speed_level * max(1, load/capacity)`; slots are shared by receive and send; files are NOT consumed on send.
- Attacks: schedule from `hours[].attacks`; the spawn window is `start = clamp(0.25*duration, 20, 60)`, `end = min(0.85*duration, duration-20)`; tier/rewards unchanged.
- Payout: exact `round(base_sum * speed * quality * multi * protocol * streak * money_mult)`; TCP x1.15, UDP x0.85; recv_fee is added on receive and is not multiplied.
- Folder: `grid.folder_dims[folder_level]`, 2 free folders at start; each extra folder costs `constants.folder_new_cost` (180 $) in prep, up to `constants.folders_max` (6). `lot_folder` buys more cells; fragmentation is not modelled, cell capacity/occupancy is.
- Shop: 3 weighted offers after each hour, `last_offer` x0.5, pity x1.5, no rerolls (locks are irrelevant), at least one affordable offer; buy the best affordable predicted-ROI lot while ROI >= 0.5 before H9, and any positive ROI in the final prep (there is no shop after H9).
- ROI proxy: `money` = next `shop[].value` step of projected remaining income; `slots`/`speed`/`channel`/`timer`/`folder`/`sla`/`shield` use measured bottlenecks from the previous hour. It is deterministic, not an oracle.

## Tuning recommendations (not applied)

- No blockers: every hour mean is inside +/-20% and the mean total is inside the income band.
### Weak lots

These lots are bought only by the final-prep money dump (no shop after H9), so their spend is not evidence of value.

- `lot_sla` (sla_cap): mean level 1.20, ROI 0.02. SLA binds only in 0/240 runs. Either lower the price/raise the effect or make timeouts hurt more.

### Best ROI is not the money lot

- Best ROI proxy is `lot_channel` (channel_capacity) at **6.87**, spend share 13.3%. `lot_money` is not the primary lever in this model, so a money-price change alone will not fix the tree coverage; check channel/slot/speed prices too.
- `lot_money` ROI is **1.09** (spend share 27.2%). It is a good buy but not front-loaded enough to justify a new price curve from this run.

## Risks and discrepancies

- Request-rate model: with the guard scoped (`first`), the request rate follows `send_interval`; early hours are limited by the player's attach+minigame+send cycle, not by the guard. The previous current-guard baseline was 9 668 $ / exit 1; this variant is 7 205 $ / exit 0.
- Not applied: `data/balance.json` is not edited by the simulator. The backend must apply guard scope `first`.
- Near the +/-20% edge: H2 -16.4%, H4 -15.3%, H5 -15.1% — seed variance can flip these hours; do not tighten further.
- Shop spend is **44.9%** of the tree, outside the 55-65% design target (not part of the exit code). The lower income of the first-only guard leaves ~4 100 $ spent instead of ~5 000 $.
- Model gaps: polyomino fragmentation/rotation is not modelled (cells only), file destruction is not modelled (the catalog grows: a successful send keeps the file), and the TCP connection is a fixed 4.2 s + 4% fail instead of a separate minigame.

## Verdict

**PASS** - every hour is within +/-20% and the mean total is inside the 6 982 - 8 500 $ band. Exit code **0**.
